# Star clusters and nuclear star clusters

Up: [[MOC - CGF 2026]] · Related: [[Reina-Campos — SCALES]], [[Partmann — IMBHs in dwarf galaxies]], [[Dynamical friction]], [[Johansson — KETJU]]

---

## The distinction

Location, mainly — and everything else follows from it.

| | Ordinary clusters | Nuclear star clusters |
|---|---|---|
| Location | anywhere | galactic centre |
| Mass | $10^2$–$10^6\,M_\odot$ | $10^6$–$10^8\,M_\odot$ |
| Density | up to $10^5\,M_\odot$ pc$^{-3}$ | $\gtrsim10^6$–$10^7$ |
| Populations | usually one | multiple, spanning Gyr |
| Metallicity spread | small | large |
| Central BH | rare / debated | usually |

**Ordinary clusters** — gravitationally bound groups formed together from one molecular cloud:
- **Open**: $10^2$–$10^3M_\odot$, young, in the disk, loosely bound, dissolve within a few hundred Myr (Pleiades)
- **Globular**: $10^4$–$10^6M_\odot$, ~12 Gyr, halo, dense, survive a Hubble time ($\omega$ Cen, M13)
- **Young massive clusters**: globular-mass objects forming *today* in starbursts and mergers → [[Reina-Campos — SCALES]], and Lahén's talk

## What makes NSCs different

**Densest stellar systems known** — more massive than any globular and more compact.

**Extended star formation histories.** An ordinary cluster forms in one event; an NSC accumulates over cosmic time through two channels:
1. Gas inflow to the centre forming stars *in situ*
2. Inspiral of globular clusters that sink by [[Dynamical friction]] and dissolve there

Both operate, and their relative importance depends on galaxy mass. Hence multiple populations and metallicity spreads.

**They contain black holes.** Globulars may host IMBHs (contested); NSCs coexist with massive BHs routinely. The MW's NSC contains Sgr A*, and the $M_{\rm NSC}$–$M_\bullet$ relation is real if scattered.

**External tidal field.** An NSC is embedded in the galaxy's own potential and fed continuously, whereas a globular is slowly stripped. Different dynamical evolution entirely.

> **Boundary case.** $\omega$ Cen and M54 are probably stripped NSCs of accreted dwarf galaxies, not true globulars. That's why they have metallicity spreads other globulars lack.

## Why people study NSCs

**Only place you can weigh a BH by watching stars orbit it.** Everything known about Sgr A*'s mass comes from tracking individual S-star orbits. The cleanest BH mass measurement in existence — every other $M_\bullet$ is inferred from gas kinematics or reverberation.

**They preserve assembly history.** A fossil record of gas inflow and cluster accretion in a way the smooth bulge isn't.

**Possible BH seed formation site.** Runaway stellar collisions in a dense young cluster is one of the main IMBH seed channels, alongside direct collapse and Pop III remnants. Live given the high-$z$ quasar timing problem — see [[Super-Eddington accretion]].

**They control BH growth and merging.**
- The NSC deepens the potential, captures gas, boosts accretion → [[Partmann — IMBHs in dwarf galaxies]]
- $F_{\rm DF}\propto M^2$, so a BH arriving at a merger *embedded* in an NSC sinks far more efficiently than a naked one. The NSC provides both the mass for dynamical friction and the stars for three-body hardening. Without one, IMBH binaries stall longer than a Hubble time.
- **Necessary ingredient for any LISA/PTA event rate prediction** → [[Johansson — KETJU]]

**Laboratories for collisional dynamics.** Mass segregation, tidal disruption events, extreme mass-ratio inspirals, stellar collisions. Rates scale steeply with density, so NSCs dominate the budget despite tiny volume.

**Scaling relations.** $M_{\rm NSC}$ correlates with host mass and with $M_\bullet$; the nucleation fraction peaks around $10^9M_\odot$ and declines at both ends. That structure demands an explanation and constrains feedback models.

**The practical reason.** The MW's NSC is close enough to resolve individual stars — the only galactic nucleus where theory can be tested star by star. Disproportionately valuable as a calibration point, like the Local Group for galaxy formation.

> **Terminology watch.** Some write NSC for nuclear *stellar* cluster, some for nuclear *star* cluster; it occasionally gets used loosely for the nuclear stellar *disc* (NSD), a different structure. If a plot axis says NSC mass and the numbers are $\sim10^9M_\odot$, they probably mean something else.

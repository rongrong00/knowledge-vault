# Huško — hybrid AGN model in COLIBRE

Up: [[MOC - CGF 2026]] · Related: [[Accretion disc states]], [[Equilibrium spin]], [[Calibration and tuning]], [[Trinca — multi-regime accretion disc]]

---

**Filip Huško** (Durham/Leiden). Huško et al. 2026, MNRAS 547. Lineage: Huško et al. 2022 on spin-driven jet feedback in idealized groups and clusters, then jets in FLAMINGO.

## The model

Three accretion disc states — **thick, thin, slim** at low, moderate and super-Eddington rates → [[Accretion disc states]] — with two feedback modes: **thermal isotropic** and **kinetic jets**.

Spin evolves through:
- gas accretion
- BH mergers
- jet spin-down
- Lense–Thirring torques
- radiation torques

**Spin sets both the jet directions and the feedback efficiencies.**

New accretion efficiencies account for the fact that most of the mass accreting through thick and slim discs is blown away in winds → [[Super-Eddington accretion]].

> It's an **alternative** to COLIBRE's fiducial thermal-only model, not a replacement. The fiducial COLIBRE remains thermal.

## The argument for the added complexity

Old two-mode prescriptions (quasar mode / radio mode) had the right qualitative structure but a hand-set threshold and hand-set efficiencies. Grounding transitions in disc theory means the Eddington-ratio boundaries, the efficiencies, and the jet-versus-thermal split all follow from $\dot m$ and spin rather than tuned parameters.

Strong physical motivation regardless of whether the observables end up distinguishable — which is exactly why the degeneracy question is the good one.

## Questions

**Best — the degeneracy question**
> COLIBRE's fiducial is thermal-only and both are calibrated to the same targets. Once you've recalibrated, is there an observable that actually distinguishes the two, or are they degenerate at the level of the calibration data?

The added complexity has to buy something. Honest answer is probably "group-scale gas fractions and the massive end," but making him say it is worth it. Lands squarely on the general problem in [[Calibration and tuning]].

**Geometry / coupling**
> Jets are launched along the spin axis. In a disk galaxy the spin often aligns with the disk angular momentum, so the jet escapes perpendicular into low-density gas. Does that mean the jets couple mostly to the CGM rather than the ISM, and is the quenching therefore a halo-scale effect?

The mechanistic reason a spin-tracking model should behave differently from isotropic thermal dumping.

**Safe openers**
> What's the spin distribution that comes out, and how does it compare to X-ray reflection measurements?

Load-bearing for the whole model, completely non-confrontational. Jet efficiency scales steeply with spin, so whether cosmological accretion is coherent (spins up) or chaotic (keeps spins low) determines everything. Reflection spins cluster high, in mild tension with chaotic accretion. → [[Equilibrium spin]]

> What fraction of BHs are in each disc state as a function of mass and redshift?

He has this figure (Fig. 3, from L200m7h) — inviting him to show something he's proud of.

**Cosmology hook**
> Does the hybrid model change halo baryon fractions enough to move the matter power spectrum suppression?

Topical: kSZ and X-ray joint analyses point to stronger gas expulsion than most simulations produce, and jet feedback is the obvious candidate. → [[Predictive power]]

**Sharper — only if the room supports it**
> Spin evolution depends on the angular momentum of accreted gas, but at COLIBRE resolution you're nowhere near the Bardeen–Petterson radius. How much of the resulting spin is set by resolved gas angular momentum versus the subgrid disc prescription, and is the spin distribution converged with resolution?

Legitimate and important, but names the model's most vulnerable assumption. Phrase as curiosity about convergence.

> You introduce accretion efficiencies because most of the mass in the thick and slim discs is blown away. Does that require compensating with higher inflow rates to keep the BH mass function right, and does it push toward a different $M_\bullet$–$M_*$ normalisation?

Shows you read the paper. Slightly in-the-weeds for Q&A.

## Cross-talk

BH **mergers** are one of the three spin-change channels here. Whether that channel matters depends on whether mergers actually complete and on what timescale — which subgrid models answer by assumption and KETJU answers by integration. → [[Johansson — KETJU]]

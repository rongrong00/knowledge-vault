# Partmann — IMBH simulations

Up: [[MOC - CGF 2026]] · Related: [[Star clusters and NSCs]], [[Dynamical friction]], [[Super-Eddington accretion]], [[Reina-Campos — SCALES]]

---

**Christian Partmann**, MPA Garching (Naab group). *Local — easy to follow up in person.*

## The work

2025 paper: dwarf galaxies ($M_{\rm baryon}\sim0.6$–$2.4\times10^8M_\odot$) at **solar-mass gas resolution** (0.5–4 $M_\odot$) with the **GRIFFIN** model — multiphase ISM, non-equilibrium low-temperature cooling, chemistry, HII regions, SNe from individual massive stars.

**Result: nuclear star clusters substantially boost IMBH growth.** The NSC deepens the potential, captures ISM gas, promotes circumnuclear disc formation, and enhances accretion.

Without an NSC, growth is feeble: a $10^4M_\odot$ seed gains only ~30–40% of its initial mass in 600 Myr. (Noctua suite finds similar.)

Related: **Partmann et al. 2024** — in the absence of an NSC, IMBH binaries in dwarfs stall for longer than a Hubble time around a parsec separation (also Biava+19, Khan+24, Zhou+25). → [[Dynamical friction]], [[Johansson — KETJU]]

## Questions

**Best — the observational discriminant**
> What's the observational discriminant? Does this predict that accreting IMBHs should be found preferentially in **nucleated** dwarfs?

Directly testable against the nucleation fraction as a function of dwarf mass. Turns the mechanism into a prediction rather than an explanation, and it's what the observers in the room want asked.

**The dynamical alternative**
> Does the NSC anchor the black hole against the fluctuating potential?

In a dwarf with bursty SN feedback the potential minimum moves around, and **wandering BHs** are a known problem in this mass regime. If the NSC's role is partly dynamical rather than purely gas-supply, that's a cleaner story.

**The resolution claim**
> Is the Bondi radius resolved at your resolution, and do you still need a boost factor?

For a $10^4M_\odot$ BH in ~10 km/s gas, $R_B \approx 0.4$ pc — marginal at solar-mass resolution. **If he can drop the boost factor entirely, that's a genuinely important claim**, since every cosmological model depends on one. → [[Calibration and tuning]]

**Cross-talk**
> Do you see super-Eddington episodes, and what duty cycle?

Connects to [[Trinca — multi-regime accretion disc]]. Also bears on whether this channel can grow seeds fast enough to matter for the high-$z$ problem.

**Sharper — better one-on-one**
> The NSC is in the initial conditions. How much of the result is the assumption that a dense nucleus pre-exists, given that NSCs and BHs probably co-evolve?

Fair, and he'll have an answer, but it questions the setup rather than the physics. Better over coffee.

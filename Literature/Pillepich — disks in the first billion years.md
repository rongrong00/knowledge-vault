# Pillepich — from nuclear stellar structures to galaxy disks in the first billion years, amid resolved SN explosions

Up: [[MOC - CGF 2026]] · Related: [[Resolution comparison]], [[Galactic winds]], [[Calibration and tuning]], [[Simulation landscape]]

---

> ⭐ **Most relevant talk of the meeting for my work.** Galaxy disks in the first billion years is the thesis topic, and Pillepich is the person most responsible for TNG50 and for the TNG disk-size and morphology analyses.

"Amid resolved SN explosions" means much higher resolution than TNG — the ideal reference point for asking whether TNG-vs-Lumina size differences are physical or resolution-driven.

## Listen for

**Resolution and convergence of the size–mass relation.** The direct question. If sizes shift systematically between her resolution and TNG's, a TNG–Lumina difference may be partly numerical. → [[Resolution comparison]]

**Rotational support vs flattening.** $V/\sigma$. High-$z$ "disks" often turn out dispersion-dominated once you look properly.

**When disks settle**, and whether that transition moves with resolution. Resolved SNe make feedback burstier, which should *delay* settling relative to TNG.

**What removing the effective equation of state does.** TNG's pressure floor puts an artificial scale on disk thickness and inner structure. This is exactly the setup to test it. → [[Calibration and tuning]]

## Questions

**Best**
> Is the size–mass relation converged, and how do your sizes compare to TNG at fixed mass and redshift?

Non-confrontational, and she'll have the plot.

**Connecting numerics to physics**
> Does the burstiness of resolved SN feedback change when disks settle relative to TNG?

Connects the resolution change to a physical outcome rather than a numerical one.

**Sharper, in-title territory**
> How much of the nuclear structure is set by the pressure floor in TNG?

## Afterward — the priority conversation of the week

She's at MPIA. Introduce yourself and say what you're comparing.

**Opener:** *"I'm comparing high-$z$ disk sizes between TNG and Lumina — at your resolution, how much of the size difference is set by resolution versus feedback?"*

She'll know immediately which TNG size systematics matter at high $z$, and that's information not available from a paper.

**If she asks what Lumina is:** same TNG physics, radiation hydrodynamics, 500 cMpc, stops at $z=3$.

**Also relevant to raise:** DM supersampling and two-body heating as a size systematic (COLIBRE uses 4× DM particles specifically for this) → [[Resolution comparison]].
